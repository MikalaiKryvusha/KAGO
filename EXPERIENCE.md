# EXPERIENCE — the agent's accumulated experience

> The agent's growing log of lessons. **Externalized memory of *what works and what doesn't*** — so a
> fresh, context-less session (or an autonomous loop) never repeats a dead end. Consult it BEFORE a task;
> append to it AFTER a meaningful attempt (success **or** failure). Grep, don't scroll.
>
> **Tags live inline on every entry** (not in a central list) — so one grep finds the experiences directly:
> `grep '#loop' EXPERIENCE.md` · `grep -i '#context\|#build' EXPERIENCE.md` · `grep '❌' -A4 EXPERIENCE.md`
> · `grep 'EXP-0007' EXPERIENCE.md`. Reuse an existing tag where one fits (grep to see what's in use).
>
> **Entry format (keep it short and grep-friendly).** Newest on top. Every entry starts with a stable id,
> an ISO date, an outcome marker (`✅` / `❌` / `❌→✅`), and inline `#tags`:
>
> ```
> ### EXP-0001 · 2026-01-01 · ✅ · #tag #area
> **Context:** one line — what was being done.
> **Tried / did:** the approach, briefly.
> **Result:** ✅/❌ — what happened.
> **Lesson:** the reusable takeaway (the reason this entry exists).   → link: bugs/NN · ideas/NN · plans/NN
> **Repro:** the ready-to-run command/check that verifies or applies the lesson — a weak session
>   executes a pasted command reliably, an essay it won't act on. REQUIRED since 2.1: a lesson
>   with no Repro line is not accepted (field-proven: lessons with a Repro command get executed,
>   essay-lessons get read and ignored). If the lesson genuinely has no command, say what to
>   OBSERVE instead — but say it as an action.
> **Trigger:** for class-level lessons — the decision point that must invoke this lesson, as
>   "writing X → run Y" (the lesson names WHERE it applies, instead of hoping to be remembered).
> **Not for:** the lesson's validity range — where it does NOT apply. A documented lesson is still a
>   hypothesis; applied outside its range it kills good ideas.
> ```
>
> **A lesson that repeats is a lesson that failed as text.** When the same class recurs in NEW code
> after its entry was recorded, the journal has proven insufficient — the lesson MUST become
> executable (a linter rule, a guard, a gate), and the entry gains the line
> `mechanized: <the tool>`. Two strikes → a mechanism, never a third reminder.
>
> The `#tags` are **trigger-tags**: before a task, grep by the task's tags and QUOTE the relevant
> lessons in your report (id + one line) — or state "no relevant lessons". An unquoted recall is
> unverifiable; `/fable-judge` checks for this line.
>

### EXP-0037 · 2026-08-10 · ❌→✅ · #measurement #thresholds #units #gates #verification
**Context:** writing the plateau detector — «temperature AND fan stable for ≥ 60 s» — as two gates per quantity: a RANGE gate for noise and a DRIFT gate for a slow climb. The drift was the median of the window's second half minus the median of its first, and the tolerance was written `PLATEAU_TEMP_DRIFT_C = 1`, read as «settles to within a degree».
**Tried / did:** wrote a selftest fixture that climbs 1.5 °C per minute — a card visibly still heating — and expected the drift gate to refuse it.
**Result:** ❌→✅ **it passed.** The halves' centres are half a window apart, so a difference of half medians over a 60 s window measures the change per 30 SECONDS: the gate labelled «1 °C» was in fact permitting 2 °C per minute, i.e. another 10 °C over the following five minutes. Rewritten as a RATE — the difference divided by the time between the two halves' CENTROIDS, in °C per minute — the number began to mean what it read, stopped depending on the window's length, and stopped depending on how densely the run was sampled (a stalled sampler had skewed the baseline too). The fixture then failed as it should.
**Lesson:** **a tolerance whose unit is implicit does not mean what it reads, and the reader who is fooled is you.** `≤ 1 °C` on a quantity derived from a window silently carries «per half-window» — a unit nobody wrote down and everybody assumes away. The general form: **any threshold on a DERIVED quantity states the unit of the derivation, or it is a different threshold than the one intended.** The cheap discriminator, and it is what caught this: write one fixture whose PHYSICAL behaviour you can state in words («this card climbs one and a half degrees a minute») and demand the verdict you would give by eye. A gate that disagrees with the sentence is measuring something else. Second half, about which unit to choose: prefer a RATE over a difference wherever the observation window is a free parameter — a rate survives changing the window, a difference silently rescales with it.   → link: `automation-engine/lib/thermal-ladder.mjs` → `judgeWindow` · `config.PLATEAU_TEMP_DRIFT_C_PER_MIN` · EXP-0034 (the same family: the unit you search in is not the unit your conclusion is about)
**Repro:** `npm run thermal -- --selftest` — the block «и число — это скорость, а не разница» asserts the fixture climbing 1.5 °C/min reports exactly 1.5, not 0.75. Mutate `judgeWindow` to divide by the window's nominal half instead of the centroid separation and the block «скорость считается между ЦЕНТРОИДАМИ половин» goes red.
**Trigger:** about to write a numeric tolerance on anything computed OVER A WINDOW (drift, slope, growth, error rate) → say the unit out loud including the denominator, and put it in the constant's NAME.
**Not for:** thresholds on directly-observed quantities (a temperature, a voltage, a count) — there the unit is the quantity's own and this ceremony buys nothing.

### EXP-0036 · 2026-08-10 · ✅ · #verification #instruments #calibration #archive #false-green
**Context:** a brand-new detector — «has the card reached thermal equilibrium?» — 46 offline blocks green, 11 mutations each reddening its own block. Everything a new guard is supposed to have, and not one byte of it had ever met real data.
**Tried / did:** before running it on the card, ran it over the THIRTEEN telemetry captures already sitting in `runs/graphics/` from earlier work — a command that costs seconds and needs no hardware (`npm run thermal -- --analyze`).
**Result:** ✅ three findings in one pass, none of which the offline suite could have produced. **(1)** The detector printed `±undefined` in its own requirements line — a renamed config key nobody dereferences in a selftest. **(2)** Its refusal message said «проб под нагрузкой 1» while the record held 113 — the counter counted window ATTEMPTS and read as a sample count, a number that lies without being wrong. **(3)** The real one: the load threshold inherited from the burst runs (`utilization ≥ 50 %`) sat exactly inside the game's own frame-boundary noise — mid-run samples read **47-49 % at 100-160 W** — so a continuous run was being chopped into 22-second pieces and a 60 s window could never form under the very load the tool exists to use. The two clusters (47-49 % at 100-160 W vs 0-22 % at 29-58 W) are only visible in real captures. And the pass produced the honest baseline as a bonus: **0 of 13 runs had ever reached a plateau**, which is the measurement that justifies the whole tool.
**Lesson:** **a new detector is calibrated against the ARCHIVE before it is trusted live — synthetic fixtures prove the logic, old real data proves the THRESHOLDS.** They are different failure classes and the offline suite cannot reach the second one: a fixture contains what its author already knew, so a constant that is wrong about the world stays green through any number of mutations. The archive is free, it is already on disk, and it carries exactly the noise, the outliers and the units the fixtures lack. Two corollaries worth keeping: **a detector run over old data must REFUSE most of it or it is decoration** — «0 of 13» is a stronger statement about the gate than any green suite; and **read the detector's own OUTPUT as a stranger** (EXP-0012), because a message that is technically true and reads as something else is a defect that only surfaces when a human reads it.   → link: `automation-engine/lib/thermal-ladder.mjs` → `analyze()` · `config.PLATEAU_LOAD_UTILIZATION_PCT` · EXP-0033 (a knob is tested the same way an instrument is)
**Repro:** `npm run thermal -- --analyze` — every capture in `runs/graphics/` judged by the plateau gates, with the shortfall named per run. Any new detector in this project gets the same treatment: point it at `runs/` before pointing it at the card.
**Trigger:** about to run a newly-written detector, guard or gate against live hardware for the first time → point it at the project's existing recorded data first and read every line of what it says.
**Not for:** projects with no archive yet, and guards over data that does not exist in recorded form (a UI state, a one-off migration) — there the fixture is all there is.

### EXP-0035 · 2026-08-10 · ❌ · #tooling #shell #argv #text #canon #mechanize
**Context:** editing Russian canon documents (STATUS, GOAL, plans) dozens of times in one session, using `node -e "…"` one-liners from bash because it felt faster than writing a script file.
**Tried / did:** passed replacement strings containing markdown backticks — ``-pl``, ``plans/05``, ``descend`` — inside a double-quoted shell argument.
**Result:** ❌ **FIVE TIMES in one session the shell EXECUTED the backticked text as a command and deleted it from the document.** Every time the exit code was 0, every time the file was written successfully, and every time the damage was a HOLE in Russian prose the owner would have read: «уровень подбирать через .», «мощности ( →», «Есть:  (закрепляет и ОСВОБОЖДАЕТ в ),  (игра)». Twice it also produced `command not found` noise that I read past. This is face 2 of the text-through-argv class the guide already documents — the one that «prints ok and the document gets HOLES, no error at all».
**Lesson:** **the rule «text travels through files, never through command-line arguments» is not about ENCODING — it is about the shell being a PROGRAM RUNNER that also rewrites its arguments.** I knew the rule, quoted it twice in commit messages the same evening, and still broke it five times, because each individual edit looked too small to deserve a file. That reasoning is the trap: the cost of the mistake is not proportional to the size of the edit. **Two strikes were supposed to mechanize it; five means the text form has failed and the fix is a HABIT WITH NO EXCEPTIONS — markdown text is edited with Write/Edit tools, and `node -e` is reserved for arithmetic and probes that produce no prose.** The one companion rule that saved every occurrence: READ THE RESULT BACK. Nothing else would have caught it.
**Repro:** `node -e "console.log('a `x` b')"` from bash — the backticked span vanishes and bash reports `x: command not found`. Then check the file: exit code 0, content mutilated.
**Trigger:** about to edit any document containing markdown, Cyrillic, or backticks → use Write/Edit, or write a script FILE. If the fingers have already typed `node -e` and the payload contains prose, stop and switch.
**Not for:** shell one-liners whose payload is ASCII arithmetic or a probe with no prose in it — those are fine and rewriting them as files is ceremony.

### EXP-0034 · 2026-08-10 · ❌→✅ · #measurement #units #search #probabilistic #gpu #verification
**Context:** the first live edge search — walk a curve offset UP in 75 MHz steps until the SDC oracle stops saying PASS, then bisect to 15 MHz. The project had never once observed a failure from undervolting, so this was the run that would answer the owner's question.
**Tried / did:** ran it, got a textbook result — PASS through +525, CRASH at +600, bisection closing on **+540 PASS / +555 CRASH**, a 15 MHz bracket. Then, before reporting it, converted the offsets into the quantity the conclusion is actually about: **volts**.
**Result:** ❌→✅ the conversion dissolved the finding and replaced it with a better one. The V/F curve is NOT linear, so +540 MHz is not 72 points of 5 mV — it moves the point serving 2842 MHz from #94 (1040 mV) to **#69 (885 mV), i.e. −155 mV**. And **+555 lands on the SAME point 69 at the SAME 885 mV.** So the two ends of the "bracket" are one voltage: at 885 mV the card passed once and crashed once. The bracket bracketed nothing — it had measured the **probabilistic edge** `researches/02` §6.4 predicts («the edge is a failure probability, not a line»), which this project had cited for weeks and never seen. Two engine defects fell out of the same conversion: it read `result.servingMv`, a field the atom does not have (storing nulls where the volts belonged), and it bisected in MHz below the resolution the hardware can express.
**Lesson:** **the unit you SEARCH in is rarely the unit your CONCLUSION is about — convert before believing, and convert before reporting.** The control variable here is a frequency offset because that is what the API accepts; the physical quantity is the voltage of the point that ends up serving the clock, and the map between them is non-linear and many-to-one. Two consequences that generalize past GPUs: **(a)** a bracket is only as fine as the underlying state distinguishes — if both ends map to the same physical state, a narrower bracket in the control variable buys ZERO resolution and reporting it as precision is a false claim; **(b)** when both ends of a bracket share a state and disagree on the verdict, that is not a broken run, it is the signature of a PROBABILISTIC boundary, and the honest report names it as such instead of picking one side. Third: an implausibly large result (−360 mV of undervolt) deserves the same suspicion as an implausibly precise one (EXP-0032) — **check the arithmetic against the real curve before the number reaches a human.**   → link: `automation-engine/engine.mjs` · STATUS fact 28 · `researches/02` §6.4 · EXP-0032
**Repro:** after any edge search, read the curve and compute `voltageForClock(cap)` at the last-PASS and first-FAIL offsets. If the two serving points are equal, the search found a probabilistic edge at that voltage, not a boundary — `npm run engine -- --search --cap 2842` now prints `bracketMv` and says so itself.
**Trigger:** about to report a search result, a threshold or a tolerance → ask "in what unit is this claim true, and is that the unit I searched in?" If they differ, convert first; if the conversion is many-to-one, check whether the bracket's ends are even distinguishable.
**Not for:** searches whose control variable IS the physical quantity (a power limit in watts, a temperature setpoint). There the conversion is the identity and this ceremony buys nothing.

### EXP-0033 · 2026-08-10 · ❌→✅ · #measurement #conditions #instruments #knobs #record #gpu
**Context:** wrapping the game load (Q2RTX timedemo) into a KAGO module. Every record needs its CONDITIONS stamped, or a later comparison is illegal (EXP-0011), so I wrote the render resolution into each record — taking it from the desktop, because in fullscreen "obviously" the engine renders at the desktop's resolution.
**Tried / did:** ran the cap-proof gate (two launches, frame cost changed by ~9×), then two more launches with `vid_geometry` requested at 1280x720 and at 1920x1080.
**Result:** ❌→✅ the gate passed loudly — 57.586 → 84.091 fps, **+46.03 %** — so the instrument does respond to its input. But the same numbers destroyed my recorded condition twice over. **(1)** At a 3840×2160 desktop the expensive frame ran **57.59 fps**, against **54.29** measured earlier at a 1280×720 desktop: nine times the pixels came out FASTER, so the load does not follow the desktop's geometry at all. **(2)** `vid_geometry` is not the knob either — 1280x720 → **57.721**, 1920x1080 → **57.283**, **0.76 % apart inside the launch's own 1.84 % scatter.** So the render size on this build is simply **not observed**: the engine's log prints no resolution line, and both candidate explanations are refuted. The record now carries three separate fields — what we REQUESTED, what the DESKTOP is, and `renderGeometryObserved: null`.
**Lesson:** **a condition you did not OBSERVE must never be stamped into a record as a condition.** A stamp exists to make later comparisons legal; a plausible stamp makes ILLEGAL comparisons look legal, which is worse than having no stamp at all — the honest `null` refuses the comparison, the invented number waves it through. Second half, and it is the reusable technique: **test a KNOB the same way you test an INSTRUMENT.** The cap proof already demanded that the FPS move when the frame cost changes; the identical discriminator applied to a setting answers "does this knob have any authority here" for one extra launch. A knob that moves the number by less than the run's own scatter is not a knob, and describing the run by it is fiction. Third half, about which way to fall: when the true value is unobservable, split the field rather than picking the most plausible occupant — "requested", "context", and "actual: unknown" are three different facts, and collapsing them is how the first draft came to assert something false while looking careful.   → link: `automation-engine/lib/graphics-load.mjs` → `buildArgs` · `researches/06` §7b · EXP-0011 · EXP-0032
**Repro:** `npm run gfx -- --run --geometry 1280x720 --runs 3` then `npm run gfx -- --run --geometry 1920x1080 --runs 3` — if the two medians differ by less than the printed within-launch spread, the setting is inert and must not be called the render size.
**Trigger:** about to write a CONDITION into a measurement record (resolution, clock, temperature, background, mode) → ask "did I OBSERVE this, or did I derive it from something plausible?" If derived — either change it and prove the number follows, or record `null` and say it is unobserved.
**Not for:** conditions that are true BY CONSTRUCTION because we set them and the tool confirms them back (the driver version, the demo file, the requested bounce-ray count). Those are observed; the rule is about values inferred from a neighbouring fact.

### EXP-0032 · 2026-08-10 · ❌→✅ · #measurement #clamp #false-precision #instruments #gpu #owner
**Context:** the first runs of a real game-like load (Q2RTX path-traced timedemo) to get an FPS number, after the throughput meter had been shown to scatter 4.3 % (EXP-0030).
**Tried / did:** ran the demo ten times in one launch, windowed, with the engine's own vsync off and its 60 fps cap removed, and read the ten printed FPS lines.
**Result:** ❌→✅ nine of ten runs printed **143.998154 fps — identical to six decimal places** — and I wrote that up as "an extraordinarily precise instrument, 0.023 % spread, two orders of magnitude better than ops/s". **The owner read the same number and said what it actually was: «144 кадра в секунду — это частота моего телевизора».** One probe settled it: rendering a ~9× cheaper frame (640×360, indirect lighting off) produced **the same 143.998154**. It was a CLAMP, not precision — the desktop compositor pacing a windowed app to the display's refresh rate, which the engine's `vid_vsync 0` does not affect. Fullscreen removed it, the variance became real (79.08 / 79.53 / 79.27 / 79.98), and the card then went to **99 % utilization, 299.97 W, `sw_power_cap`, 77 °C** — the first genuine saturation this project has produced.
**Lesson:** **impossibly good repeatability is a SYMPTOM, not an achievement — a clamped quantity agrees with itself perfectly.** The tell was in my own data and I read it upside down: six identical decimals across runs is not what a noisy physical system produces, and the moment a measurement looks better than the instrument has any right to be, the first hypothesis must be that something is holding it, not that we got lucky. The cheap discriminator costs one run: **make the work dramatically cheaper (or dearer) and see whether the number MOVES.** A number that ignores a ninefold change in its input is not measuring its input. Second half, about scope: this also means **a graphics load must first be PROVEN not to be capped before any of its numbers mean anything** — vsync in the engine is not the only pacer; the compositor, the driver profile and the display all cap independently, and only fullscreen plus a cost-sweep rules them out. Third half, about who caught it: the owner spotted it from the raw number alone because he knew his own hardware — 144 Hz was his TV. **A domain fact the human holds can beat the agent's whole analysis, so print the RAW numbers into the report rather than only the conclusions drawn from them.**   → link: `researches/06` §7b · STATUS fact 23 · EXP-0030
**Repro:** run the load twice with the per-frame cost changed by a large factor (resolution and/or ray count). If the FPS is within noise of identical, it is capped — for Q2RTX: `+set vid_fullscreen 1` is mandatory, plus `cl_maxfps 0` (its default 60 caps too) and `drs_enable 0` (dynamic resolution scaling silently retargets the load).
**Trigger:** about to report a repeatability figure that looks excellent, or a benchmark result from a windowed graphics app → change the workload's cost by a large factor and require the number to move before believing it.
**Not for:** quantities that are exact by construction — a checksum, a frame count, a clock snapped to a hardware grid. Those SHOULD be identical, and identity there is evidence rather than suspicion.

### EXP-0031 · 2026-08-10 · ❌→✅ · #limits #binding-constraint #measurement #gpu #method
**Context:** the shipped profile's shape — raise the whole V/F curve so every frequency needs less voltage, and cap the top so the card cannot spend the saving on speed. The cap was placed at the curve's own highest frequency (3172 MHz), which is where "don't let it go above stock" naturally reads.
**Tried / did:** wrote the vector, cooled both sides to the same temperature, measured stock against undervolted at that cap.
**Result:** ❌→✅ **zero saving: 137.3 → 137.1 W, while the clock went 2887 → 2932 MHz.** The cap was correct and completely inert, because *the card never reaches the curve's top under load* — it sat at 2887 of a 3172 ceiling, limited by its own boost algorithm rather than by the curve. A limit placed where the system does not operate constrains nothing, so the freed headroom went into frequency at unchanged voltage. Moving the cap to the OPERATING clock (2887, measured on the stock side of the same run) produced −10.53 W immediately; placing it 30 MHz higher gave the stock clock delivered exactly, for −7.71 W.
**Lesson:** **a cap only does work where the system actually operates — so place a limit at the MEASURED operating point, never at the theoretical maximum.** The trap is that the top-of-range cap is the one that sounds safest and reads best in prose ("nothing above stock"), and it is the one that does nothing. The general form: before relying on a constraint, ask what is CURRENTLY binding — here the binding constraint was the card's voltage/boost algorithm, not the curve's ceiling, and until the cap was moved below that binding point it could not participate at all. Second half, which is why this cost one run rather than a day: the experiment printed the delivered clock next to the watts, so "speed instead of savings" was visible in one line. **Instrument the mechanism, not only the outcome** — a run that had reported only watts would have said "no effect" and taught nothing about why.   → link: `plans/05` §4.1 · `researches/02` §6.2 · `buildRaiseAndCapVector`
**Repro:** `npm run vfstep -- --shape --mhz 45 --cap-at 2917` — expect the delivered clock to equal stock's and the watts to fall by more than `config.POWER_METER_SPREAD_W`. Re-run with the cap at the curve's top and the watts must NOT fall while the clock rises: that is the inert-cap signature.
**Trigger:** about to bound a system with a cap, limit, quota or ceiling → measure where it actually operates first, and place the bound relative to THAT, not to the range's end.
**Not for:** safety limits, whose whole job is to sit at the edge and never bind in normal operation (the hardware's ±1000 MHz offset range is exactly that, and it is correct there).
**SECOND STRIKE — 2026-08-10 20:1x, same class, other end of the same lever.** The first instance was a CONSTRAINT placed where nothing operates; this one is a GAIN delivered in a quantity that does not bind. Under a real path-traced game the same raised curve bought **+38 MHz of delivered clock at unchanged 300 W** — and **zero frames: 56.801 → 56.605 FPS, −0.35 % against a 0.90 % instrument floor.** More of a thing the system was not short of buys nothing, exactly as a limit on a thing the system does not reach constrains nothing. **The general form, now stated once for both ends: FIND WHAT BINDS, then act on THAT — a lever is worth only as much as the constraint it touches.** Under compute the binding constraint was voltage/boost, so the raise showed up as watts; under the game it is the 300 W power cap plus something that is not core clock, so the raise showed up as nothing. **Mechanized rather than re-remembered:** `runShapeExperiment` now prints the price in the load's OWN unit (FPS for graphics, ops/s for compute) and judges every delta against that instrument's measured floor, so a gain that bought nothing reports itself as noise instead of being written up as a win.

### EXP-0030 · 2026-08-10 · ❌ · #measurement #instruments #statistics #baseline #throughput
**Context:** judging the price of a profile in the owner's formula («снижаем потребление, пока цена ≤ N»), where the price is throughput — ops/s from the workload's own counter, cross-checked against `utilization.gpu` when the meter was built (EXP-0017).
**Tried / did:** compared the undervolted side's ops/s against the stock side's, inside one run, exactly as the earlier power delta was compared — and phase 2 had measured the price's scatter at **0.18 %**, which made a 1 % difference look meaningful.
**Result:** ❌ the two STOCK sides of two runs 40 minutes apart read **15.58 and 14.94 ×10¹² ops/s — 4.3 % apart** — while their watts agreed within **0.15 %** and their delivered clock was **identical** (2887 MHz both times). So the stock baseline alone moves several times further than the effect being measured, and the same pair of runs produced "−0.95 %" and "−4.3 %" prices for undervolted sides that themselves agreed within 0.8 %. The 0.18 % figure is a WITHIN-series number and had been quietly promoted to a cross-run one.
**Lesson:** **each quantity has its OWN scatter, and a spread measured for one may not be borrowed for another that rides in the same record.** Watts, temperature, clock and throughput were captured by one command into one JSON, which is exactly what makes the borrowing feel legitimate — they arrive together, so they seem equally trustworthy. They are not: here the clock was exact, the power agreed to 0.15 %, and the throughput wandered 4.3 %. This is EXP-0018's within-series trap on a new quantity, and the operational consequence is specific: **state the claim on the observable that can carry it.** "No performance loss" is defensible about the DELIVERED CLOCK, which was directly observed to be identical; it is not defensible about ops/s until two independent series bound that meter's own cross-run range.   → link: `plans/05` §4.1 and P5-AC8 · `config.POWER_METER_SPREAD_PCT` · EXP-0018
**Repro:** `npm run power -- --capture --workload sdc_fma --seconds 30 --sustain 30 --label priceA --repeat 5`, then the same with `--label priceB` in a separate session, then `npm run power -- --spread priceA`, `--spread priceB` and the pooled `--spread price`. Quote the POOLED range for ops/s beside any price claim; if it exceeds the claim, the claim is not available.
**Trigger:** about to quote a percentage difference for one field of a multi-field measurement record → find the scatter measured for THAT field across independent runs; never inherit a neighbouring field's floor.
**Not for:** fields whose exactness is structural rather than statistical — a delivered clock snapping to a hardware grid, a checksum, a count — those need no spread.

### EXP-0029 · 2026-08-10 · ❌→✅ · #reporting #silent-failure #control-flow #verification
**Context:** the fan-write tool — a try/finally around a GPU write, the rollback in the `finally`, and the block list printed after the whole construct.
**Tried / did:** added early `return 1` guards inside the `try` for the refusal paths (level below the card's floor, a write the API rejected, the card already colder than the setpoint), which is the obvious shape for "stop here".
**Result:** ❌→✅ caught before it bit, by reading the control flow rather than the output: **an early `return` inside a `try` runs the `finally` and then leaves the function — so every one of those paths exited with the block list UNPRINTED.** The rollback still ran (it lives in the `finally`), but a REFUSED WRITE would have reported nothing at all: no blocks, no ИТОГ, just an exit code. The successful path printed beautifully, which is exactly why this survives review. Fixed by moving the report INTO the `finally`, after the rollback block is pushed — the only placement no return can bypass.
**Lesson:** **a summary placed after a try/finally is a summary the failure paths skip, and the failure paths are the ones that needed it.** The tell is structural and greppable: a `return` inside a `try` whose function prints its findings afterwards. Two consequences worth generalizing past printing: the report belongs where the CLEANUP belongs, because both are things that must happen on every path; and a tool whose happy path is verbose while its refusals are mute trains its reader to treat silence as success — the same failure family as a crashed verifier reading like a green one (EXP-0016, second half). This project's own convention already points the right way: the rollback lives in a `finally` precisely because every path must run it, and the report has exactly the same requirement.   → link: `automation-engine/lib/nvapi.mjs` → `mainFanWrite` · `plans/04` §4.7
**Repro:** `npm run nvapi -- --fan-write 20` (below this card's 30 % floor) must print its refusal block and `ИТОГ`, not just exit 1. Audit the class: `grep -n "return [01];" <file>` inside a `try` whose function prints after the `finally`.
**Trigger:** about to add an early `return` inside a `try` in a tool that prints a block list / summary → check where that printing lives; if it is after the try/finally, move it inside the `finally` first.
**Not for:** functions that return data to a caller which does the printing — there the caller's path is the one to check, and an early return is perfectly fine.

### EXP-0028 · 2026-08-10 · ❌→✅ · #readback #instruments #mechanical #observation #gpu #generalization
**Context:** the project's first fan write (manual level 60 % through NvAPI's client fan-cooler interface), verified by the rule this project already owned — EXP-0014: *a written value counts as read back only when two consecutive samples agree.*
**Tried / did:** wrote the level, polled `nvidia-smi fan.speed` until two consecutive samples agreed, and asserted the settled value was at least the card's floor.
**Result:** ❌→✅ green, and **wrong**. Two runs of the IDENTICAL command disagreed by a factor of two: read ~2 s after the write the coolers reported **768 / 824 / 768 rpm** (`fan.speed` 30 %), read ~14 s after it they reported **1856 / 1861 / 1877 rpm** (61 %, matching the commanded 60 %). **Repro:** `npm run nvapi -- --fan-write 60` — the block «скорость ДОШЛА до заказанных 60 %» must report a value within 8 pp of 60 and name how many probes it took (measured: 12 probes ≈ 8 s). A run that reports 30 % and passes means the target requirement has regressed.
**Trigger:** about to verify a write by reading a value back → name what KIND of quantity it is; if it can ramp, drift or settle, require the target and a physics-sized timeout, not just agreement.
**Not for:** discrete state a driver flips (a lock, a mode, a limit, a bit) — there agreement IS the proof and adding a target check buys nothing.

### EXP-0024 · 2026-08-10 · ❌→✅ · #api #silent-failure #status-codes #verification #reverse-engineering #gpu
**Context:** KAGO's first ADDRESSED write to the V/F curve — one point, one mask bit — after the record's stride, offset field and unit had all been measured.

**Not for:** APIs that genuinely validate indices (most documented ones do), and cases where the state legitimately cannot move — here a negative offset on a point already at the clock floor writes correctly and changes no frequency, which is a THIRD outcome that must be told apart from both success and no-op.

The second half is the causality rule: **with N=1 for a condition, name the CORRELATION and not the cause** — a co-varying condition (fan, background, order) will happily explain a difference until one more run refutes it, and a cause written into a comment or a doc outlives the data that never supported it. **Repro:** `npm run power -- --capture --workload branchy --seconds 30 --sustain 30 --repeat 5 --label stockA`, then the same with `--label stockB`, then `npm run power -- --spread stockA`, `--spread stockB` and the pooled `--spread stock`. **Trigger:** about to state an instrument's precision, a benchmark's noise floor, or a "no measurable difference" → take a SECOND independent series before quoting the number, and pool them.
**Not for:** measurements whose conditions genuinely cannot drift between series (a pure function timed on fixed input, a checksum), and one-shot observations where no repetition exists — there, say the figure is single-sourced instead of inventing a spread.
